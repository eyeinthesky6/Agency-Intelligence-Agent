# Competitor Marketing & GTM Intelligence — Kazam Energy

> Date: 2026-08-19
> Competitors: Statiq, Bolt.Earth, ChargeZone
> Purpose: agency strategy prep, not product-feature benchmarking

## Executive read

- **The category is multi-sided.** The major players are simultaneously marketing to drivers, enterprises/fleets, properties/site owners and expansion/channel partners.
- **Use-case segmentation is increasingly standard.** Bolt.Earth and Statiq are particularly explicit about workplace, enterprise, RWA/property, fleet and partner/investor journeys.
- **Partner/site-capital acquisition is a real GTM motion.** Statiq uses FOCO/franchise, ChargeZone has a franchise funnel, Bolt.Earth recruits distributors, and Kazam has partner/dealer intake.
- **Proof is a competitive weapon.** Bolt has a strong named enterprise case-study library; Kazam has strong quantified operating proof, often anonymized; all players emphasize scale/network/partnership credentials.
- **Competitors are educating buyers to create demand.** Franchise guides, charging-business explainers, workplace/RWA guides and fleet electrification content are not merely SEO—they reduce sales friction before a sales call.
- **Kazam's likely whitespace is not more generic EV content.** It is clearer buyer-specific paths, stronger case-study packaging, partner-segment acquisition and proprietary operational/category intelligence.

# Competitor playbooks

## Statiq

### Apparent GTM intent

Statiq is deliberately making **infrastructure ownership and deployment** understandable to non-charging specialists.

Signals:

- dedicated franchise proposition
- FOCO model explained clearly
- direct-ownership option for hotels, fleets, workplaces, fuel stations and commercial properties
- fleet infrastructure pages
- content explaining how charging-station businesses make money
- local station announcements

### What this suggests

Statiq is not waiting only for enterprise procurement teams. It is building an acquisition engine for people/businesses that control useful sites and capital.

### Agency lesson for Kazam

Do not copy FOCO unless it fits Kazam's actual model. But if Kazam relies on dealers, installers, site owners or implementation partners, each partner class should have a clear proposition and funnel rather than a generic partner form.

---

## Bolt.Earth

### Apparent GTM intent

Bolt behaves like a company trying to **own search/use-case intent across many physical environments**.

Signals:

- dedicated enterprise/workplace/RWA/builder/parking pages
- separate B2B contact journeys
- distributor acquisition
- product/hardware pages
- named enterprise case studies
- downloadable/educational enterprise assets

### What this suggests

Bolt is turning each buyer context into its own discoverable demand surface and then reducing risk with recognizable proof.

### Agency lesson for Kazam

Kazam can benchmark the structure—not necessarily the messaging. Its high-value buyers (OEM, fleet, CPO, utility, fuel-retail, leasing) deserve equally clear buyer journeys and proof architecture.

---

## ChargeZone

### Apparent GTM intent

ChargeZone combines three distinct commercial stories:

1. **consumer/intercity charging**
2. **fleet electrification**
3. **franchise/site investment**

Additional signal: ChargeClub loyalty/rewards creates a direct consumer retention layer, separate from enterprise sales.

### What this suggests

ChargeZone is comfortable operating multiple GTM motions under one brand as long as each has a clear customer/job.

### Agency lesson for Kazam

Breadth itself is not the problem. The problem arises when different audiences are forced through the same narrative. Kazam can keep a broad platform if the buyer paths are explicitly separated.

# Repeated GTM patterns

## Table stakes

### 1. Big proof

Network/deployment/session/customer scale, recognizable logos and ecosystem partnerships appear throughout the category.

**For Kazam:** its strong metrics and product outcomes should be visible at the exact point where each buyer needs proof.

### 2. Buyer/use-case landing pages

The category increasingly organizes around jobs such as:

- workplace charging
- fleet charging
- property/RWA
- franchise/site ownership
- enterprise network management
- CPO operations

**For Kazam:** buyer job should be primary; internal product modules should support the story.

### 3. Partner-led expansion

Franchise, dealer, distributor and site partnerships appear repeatedly.

**For Kazam:** identify which partner class actually matters economically before building a dedicated acquisition system.

### 4. Education before conversion

Guides explain implementation, business models, charging types, ownership and operational questions.

**For Kazam:** content should answer high-value pre-sales questions for fleets/OEMs/CPOs/utilities, not merely publish EV news.

# Winning-looking patterns

## Named customer proof

Bolt's Optum, Bank of America and SAP examples make the enterprise story tangible.

**Why it looks useful:** enterprise buyers can see a peer organization's implementation rather than trusting abstract claims.

**Kazam implication:** where permissions exist, convert quantified anonymized examples into named, buyer-specific stories.

## Partner acquisition as a productized funnel

Statiq/ChargeZone explain the commercial model, process and next step before the lead form.

**Why it looks useful:** it self-qualifies intent and reduces ambiguity.

**Kazam implication:** if partner recruitment is important, explain each partner type's role, economics/value and onboarding path.

## Use-case SEO / intent capture

Bolt has a large footprint around specific environments and business types.

**Why it looks useful:** high-intent prospects can land directly in their problem context.

**Kazam implication:** map SEO/content to fleet/OEM/CPO/utility/leasing problems rather than generic EV keywords.

# Potential white spaces for Kazam

## 1. Data-led category authority

Kazam has an unusually broad operating data footprint across chargers, fleets, sessions, tariffs and energy.

Competitors visibly emphasize network/deployment scale; fewer public surfaces turn operating data into regular category intelligence.

**Hypothesis:** Kazam could create a distinctive authority asset around aggregated charging/energy operations.

Possible themes:

- charger uptime benchmarks
- fleet utilisation patterns
- tier-2/3 charging behavior
- multi-OEM reliability
- charging-session economics
- energy-management patterns
- interoperability adoption

Only use data that can be safely aggregated and published.

## 2. Segment-specific proof libraries

Create separate proof for:

- OEM home charging
- quick-commerce/fleet
- fuel-retail/multi-site network
- CPO/revenue management
- utility/energy management
- leasing operations

This is more sales-useful than one general case-study library.

## 3. Partner segmentation

A partner/dealer page exists, but the company may have multiple partner jobs:

- dealer/reseller
- installer/service partner
- site/property partner
- charging-network/CPO integration partner
- technology/OEM partner

**Hypothesis:** separate journeys may improve partner quality if these groups are strategically important.

# What NOT to infer yet

- that Kazam needs a franchise business
- that Kazam's current site converts poorly
- that Bolt's SEO produces better revenue
- that social cadence determines market share
- that tier-2/3 should receive more budget

Those require internal data.

# Questions for the client

1. Which customer segments contribute the most qualified pipeline today?
2. Which of the broad product lines are actually growth priorities?
3. Which competitors appear most often in live deals?
4. Which partner types generate real revenue/deployments?
5. Which content/pages already influence sales conversations?
6. What percentage of pipeline is inbound vs partnerships vs founder/enterprise sales vs events/channel?
7. Which customer stories can be named publicly?
8. Which tier-2/3 markets are strategic rather than simply reporting high activity?
