# Pricing Strategy — Sintercom India Limited

> Date: 2026-08-19
> Public-data scenario analysis. This is not a quote and does not infer confidential customer pricing.

## 1. Pricing thesis

Sintercom should treat pricing as **four linked negotiations**:

1. **piece economics** — material + conversion + secondary operations + contribution;
2. **tooling / engineering economics** — who pays for development, tooling and changes;
3. **cash economics** — inventory, receivable days, safety stock, capacity reservation and financing;
4. **customer value** — the savings/performance/localization benefit versus forging, machining, import or another design.

For a company with significant working-capital intensity and large customer programs, optimising only `₹/part` is insufficient.

---

## 2. What the customer is really buying

### Legacy PM components

The buyer is not buying metal powder. They may be buying:

- lower material waste
- fewer machining steps
- repeatability at volume
- lower assembled weight
- part consolidation
- stable series production
- an already-qualified single-source component

### Export / localization program

The buyer may also value:

- Indian localization
- reduced landed freight/import exposure
- supply-chain resilience
- local quality/engineering response
- compliance with localization requirements

### SMC motor components

The buyer may value:

- motor topology/performance enabled by SMC
- compact geometry/design freedom
- localized series manufacturing
- faster transition from design/prototype to volume
- secure access to a qualified licensed manufacturing route

This value determines price ceiling/headroom; seller cost determines the floor.

## 3. Buyer economic alternative

For each RFQ, sales should record the incumbent alternative:

| Dimension | Current alternative | Sintercom proposal | Evidence required |
|---|---|---|---|
| Process | forging / machining / casting / import / other PM | PM / SMC / localized PM | current process |
| Material yield | unknown | near-net-shape advantage possible | input vs finished weight |
| Secondary operations | unknown | potentially reduced | operation count/cost |
| Tooling | existing / new | PM tooling | annualised tooling cost |
| Weight | current | proposed | drawing / design |
| Quality/rejection | current | proposed | PPM/rejection data |
| Lead time | current | proposed | supplier logistics |
| Freight/import | current | localized | landed-cost data |
| Inventory | current | proposed | MOQ / shipment cadence |
| Unit cost | current | proposed | customer quote / should-cost |

Do not promise savings until engineering validates the conversion.

---

## 4. Cost-to-serve architecture

For every major quote, build:

```text
metal powder / alloy
+ compaction and sintering conversion
+ heat treatment / sizing / machining / secondary operations
+ tooling amortisation
+ quality / inspection / traceability
+ packaging
+ freight / export handling
+ warranty / rejection provision
+ engineering / application support
+ committed safety stock carrying cost
+ receivables financing cost
+ FX / commodity exposure not passed through
= economic cost to serve
```

Then apply target contribution/margin.

### Critical missing inputs

Public data cannot provide:

- powder grade/kg cost by part
- cycle/press/furnace utilisation
- scrap/rejection by part
- tool life
- customer payment terms
- actual freight/incoterms
- agreed raw-material price variation mechanism

Management must supply these before an exact price recommendation is possible.

## 5. Tenneco program — scenario

Public reporting gives an estimated total contract value of ₹153.2 crore over three years.

If distributed evenly, this is ~₹51.1 crore per year. The actual schedule may be different.

### Working-capital sensitivity

If that annualised revenue required incremental net funded exposure of:

| Incremental funded days | Approx. exposure |
|---:|---:|
| 60 days | ₹8.4 crore |
| 90 days | ₹12.6 crore |
| 120 days | ₹16.8 crore |

This matters because CRISIL already reported high inventory/receivable days and ~95% bank-limit utilisation before this ramp.

### Negotiation priorities

For a program this material, explicitly protect:

- raw-material index/pass-through
- USD/INR FX mechanism if applicable
- committed volume / take-or-pay band
- forecast freeze period
- batch size and shipment cadence
- customer-funded or priced safety stock
- payment days / LC / export-credit protection
- tooling/NRE ownership
- engineering change costs
- cancellation/obsolescence terms
- freight/incoterms

### Walk-away warning

A high-revenue order with weak contribution and 120+ incremental funded days can create more financing stress than value. The commercial team should report **cash-adjusted contribution** alongside nominal gross margin for major programs.

## 6. Pricing corridor

Without confidential cost and customer-value inputs, use a corridor rather than an invented unit price.

### Floor

Covers:

- full variable/manufacturing cost
- committed tooling/NRE recovery
- customer-specific quality/engineering
- incremental logistics
- expected inventory/receivable financing burden
- unpassed FX/material risk
- minimum acceptable contribution

### Target

Floor plus strategic return for:

- capacity consumed
- complexity
- quality risk
- single-source responsibility
- engineering support
- customer concentration

### Stretch / value price

Justified only when Sintercom can quantify customer value such as:

- eliminated machining operations
- higher material yield
- localization vs imported part
- weight/performance benefit
- assembly simplification
- unique licensed/SMC capability
- shorter lead time / resilience

### Walk-away / re-open terms

Re-price or reject where:

- raw materials/FX are unprotected
- volume is non-committed but price assumes scale
- safety stock sits entirely on Sintercom
- receivable days exceed the economics embedded in price
- customer price-down exceeds achievable productivity gain
- engineering changes are free/unbounded

## 7. Recommended RFQ / negotiation architecture

### Stage A — feasibility

Customer provides drawing/application, current process, annual volume, material/performance requirements and incumbent pain.

### Stage B — prototype / development

Charge or explicitly amortise:

- engineering
- prototypes
- tooling
- validation

Do not bury all development cost inside an optimistic volume forecast without protection.

### Stage C — series quote

Present:

- base unit price at committed volume band
- raw-material/FX adjustment method
- additional volume bands
- tooling recovery status
- logistics/incoterms
- payment terms
- annual productivity assumptions

### Stage D — value case

Where process conversion is involved, attach a one-page TCO comparison that shows what the customer saves versus the current manufacturing route.

---

## 8. Sales / marketing proof needed

The agency can materially improve pricing power by building evidence, not ads.

Priority assets:

1. **PM Conversion Economics Calculator** — current process vs PM.
2. **Three technical case studies** — engineering problem → process change → economic/performance result.
3. **SMC design/value guide** — when SMC wins and when it does not.
4. **Localization/TCO worksheet** — landed/imported vs local series supply.
5. **RFQ qualification form** — filters out uneconomic low-volume/bad-fit parts before engineering time is spent.

These assets reduce price-only comparisons.

---

## 9. Management questions

1. Which top-10 products have the highest contribution after working-capital financing?
2. Which customers receive material pass-through and which leave Sintercom exposed?
3. What annual price-down is contractually expected by major OEMs?
4. What is the minimum annual volume by part complexity at which PM typically beats the incumbent process?
5. Who owns tooling and what happens if customer volume falls short?
6. What is the actual funded-day requirement of the Tenneco program?
7. How much of SMC/EV work is priced as engineering/NRE versus series unit price?
8. Does commercial approval currently include cash-cycle and concentration risk, or only unit margin?
